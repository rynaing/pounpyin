Typed memory books were built for grandparents who write emails in English. Pounpyin is built for ours — the ones who crossed an ocean, who cook from memory, whose best stories have never been written down. And it starts with the one thing every elder already knows how to do: talk.

How it works

Three steps. Stories for generations.

1

A weekly prompt, in their language

Every week a thoughtful question arrives by gentle push notification — written Burmese-first, with English alongside. No App Store, no accounts for them to manage: it installs straight from the web to the home screen.

2

They answer by voice

One tap to record, two minutes of talking. No typing, no passwords, no tiny keyboards. If they miss a week, the prompt waits patiently — reminders nudge, never nag.

3

An audio archive, forever

Every answer is saved as audio — their voice, their laugh, their pauses — with a transcript beside it. Grandchildren will hear these stories exactly as they were told.

Questions

Everything families ask us

Is it really in Burmese?

Yes — every prompt is written Burmese-first, then English alongside it. Answers can be spoken in whichever language feels natural. This is the whole point of Pounpyin.

My parent doesn't use apps. Will this work?

That's exactly who we built it for. There's nothing to download from an app store — it installs straight from the web to the home screen, and answering is one tap to record. No accounts, no passwords, no typing.

How do the reminders work?

A gentle push notification each week when a new prompt arrives — and a soft nudge if a prompt sits unanswered. They remind; they never nag.

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes. On iPhone, tap Share → "Add to Home Screen" and Pounpyin behaves like an app, including reminders. On Android, you'll get an install prompt right on this page.

What if they miss a few weeks?

Life happens. Missed prompts wait patiently. Even twenty answered prompts make a treasure — and every one is in their own voice.

Who can hear the stories?

Only your family. Recordings are private to your family's account, always. Nothing is shared, sold, or made public.

What about Karen, Chin, or other languages?

We start with Burmese and English, because that's where we can do it well — and the app is built to add languages from day one. Karen and Chin communities are a priority for what's next; many of those stories are the most urgent to preserve.

When does it launch?

We're building now. Join the waitlist and you'll be first to know — founding families help shape the prompt library.
